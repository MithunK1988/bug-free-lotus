A procedure that no longer applies is a hazard. Somebody will find it, it will look maintained, and
they will follow it.

## Delete or withdraw

[d360]: # 'table border="all" striped="true"'

| | Deleting the file | Marking it withdrawn |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Readers | Cannot reach it | Can reach it and see it is withdrawn |
| Old links | Break, unless redirected | Still work |
| The record | Kept in the history | Kept in the history and on the page |
| Suits | Content that was never in force | A procedure people were trained on |

[d360]: # "/table"

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Deleting the file does not delete the evidence**
>
> The history still holds every version and every approval. This is the point people most often get
> wrong on arriving from a filesystem, and it is the reason retirement here is a lighter decision
> than it used to be.

## Doing it

[d360]: # 'multi-level-list styles="decimal,lower-alpha"'

1. **Say what replaces it**, with a link, or say plainly that nothing does.
2. **Decide what the address should do** — a withdrawn page, or a redirect to the replacement.
3. **Find what links to it** and fix what you can reach.
4. **Tell the people who were following it.** This is the step that gets missed.
5. **Make the change the normal way**, reviewed and approved like any other.

[d360]: # "/multi-level-list"

[d360]: # 'divider style="dashed"'

---

[d360]: # 'callout kind="error"'

> **Never retire a procedure by quietly removing it from the navigation**
>
> The page stays live, the address still works, search may still find it, and nothing on it says it
> is out of force. It is the most dangerous state a controlled document can be in and it looks like
> housekeeping.

[d360]: # "/callout"

[d360]: # 'callout kind="success"'

> **Retiring procedures is a sign of a healthy manual**
>
> A manual that only grows becomes one nobody trusts, because readers cannot tell which half is
> current. Withdrawal is maintenance, not failure.

[d360]: # "/callout"

> [!TIP]
> **Review retirements as carefully as changes**
>
> Taking a procedure out of force is a decision with the same weight as putting one in. Same path,
> same approver, same record.
