This is a **<span data-variable="productType">{{variable.productType}}</span>** project set up for
user manuals and standard operating procedures. Your documents are files in a Git repository, and the
repository is the record of what they said and who changed them.

[d360]: # 'snippet name="deleteMe"'

## Why a repository suits controlled documents

> [!NOTE]
> **You already needed everything Git does**
>
> A controlled document needs an unalterable history, a named author on every change, a review step
> before it takes effect, and the ability to show what the procedure said on a given date. A
> repository gives you all four as a side effect of how it works.

## Your first hour

- [ ] Look at the repository and find one procedure.
- [ ] Open its history and read one past change.
- [ ] Make a change and take it through review.
- [ ] Decide who signs procedures off.

> [!TIP]
> **Settle who approves before you write anything**
>
> A procedure without a named approver is a draft with a confident tone. The approval step is the
> whole difference between a document and a controlled document, and it is a people decision rather
> than a tooling one.
