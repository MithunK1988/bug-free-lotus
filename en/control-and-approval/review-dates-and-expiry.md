A review date belongs in the file, where it is visible, diffable and impossible to lose — rather than
in a spreadsheet that one person maintains.

## In the front matter

```yaml
status:
  expiresAt: 2027-05-13
```

Changing that date is a change like any other: it is reviewed, it is approved, and the history shows
who moved it and when.

[d360]: # 'callout kind="success"'

> **Moving a review date becomes a visible act**
>
> In a spreadsheet, pushing a date out is a silent edit. Here it is a diff somebody approves, which
> is a small change with a surprisingly large effect on how often it happens.

[d360]: # "/callout"

## Which procedures get one

[d360]: # 'table border="all" striped="true"'

| Give a review date to | Leave alone |
| :--- | :--- |
| Anything with a legal or regulatory basis | A page explaining how to use the manual |
| Anything quoting a limit, a figure or a contact | An overview that rarely changes |
| Anything referencing equipment or a supplier | Sample content like this page |

[d360]: # "/table"

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A review date on every page is a review date nobody honours**
>
> Two hundred expiring documents is a quarterly alert everybody dismisses. Twenty is a list somebody
> works through.

[d360]: # "page-break"

## Making the date do something

[d360]: # 'multi-level-list styles="decimal,lower-alpha"'

1. **Decide what expiry means** before you set any dates.
   1. A prompt to look, which is the usual answer.
   2. A banner on the page, which is a stronger one.
   3. Withdrawal, which is rare and should be deliberate.
2. **Give the list an owner**, by name.
3. **Put the review in a calendar**, because an alert without a slot is an alert that is dismissed.

[d360]: # "/multi-level-list"

> [!CAUTION]
> **An expired procedure still in force is worse than no date at all**
>
> It documents that you knew it needed checking and did not. If you are not going to act on the
> dates, do not set them.

> [!NOTE]
> **A review is not automatically a change**
>
> "Looked at it, still correct, next review in a year" is a perfectly good outcome — as long as it is
> recorded as a change to the dates, so the history shows the review happened.
